Could the US power grid handle 100% electric cars?

Americans drove a record 3.28 trillion miles in 2024 (FHWA). At roughly 0.3 kWh per mile, an all-electric fleet would need about 1,000 TWh a year, about a fifth more than the record 4,430 TWh the US generated in 2025 (EIA). The twist: most cars would charge overnight, when plants sit idle, and a 2006 Pacific Northwest National Laboratory study found spare off-peak capacity could fuel about 84% of US vehicles as plug-in hybrids. The weak spot is local: PNNL (2020) notes one fast-charging car can draw as much as up to 50 homes. Would you charge at night to keep the lights on?
