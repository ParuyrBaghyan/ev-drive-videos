A brand-new electric car has already caused more pollution than a brand-new gas car, before it drives a single mile. So are EVs really better for the planet? We follow the carbon from the mine to the factory, the road and the scrapyard: battery manufacturing, lithium and cobalt mining, the gas car's hidden mountain of CO2, the break-even point, why your power grid changes everything, three popular myths, battery recycling, and what the big lifecycle studies actually conclude, including where EVs don't win.

US EPA: 8,887 g CO2 per gallon of gasoline; typical driver ~11,500 miles/year
AIDA (environmental group): ~150 m3 fresh water per tonne of lithium in Andean salt flats
Adamas Intelligence (2025): cobalt per battery-electric car down 24% to 3.3 kg; DRC share of cobalt supply
Recurrent (2023): ~1.5% of 15,000 EVs had non-recall battery replacements
Ricardo brake-emissions study: e-Golf 0.3–1.2 mg/km vs ICE 2–3 mg/km PM2.5 from brakes
US Dept. of Energy: EV ~87–91% efficient vs ~30% for gasoline cars
Redwood Materials pilot (2023): 95%+ recovery of key battery metals
ICCT global lifecycle study (2021): EVs 66–69% lower in Europe, 60–68% US, 37–45% China, 19–34% India
